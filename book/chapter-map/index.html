Part I · Why It Keeps Failing
Scene 1 · Kickoffdrafted

Day one of one disguised engagement: Marcus (COO), Elena (CFO), fourteen initiatives, nine open decisions, the four-month decision. Names and counts are placeholders until the vignette sessions.

1Transformation Theatredrafted

Argues: transformation fails at the leadership layer before it fails anywhere else.

Inside: the PIR scene · theatre vs reality · governance theatre · “negligent, not ambitious” · the first “No.” · AI as amplifier, one paragraph.

Tool: Clarity Test. Voice: none. From: Ch 1.

2Strategy Is What You Funddrafted

Argues: a strategy is what you fund at the expense of something else; most transformation strategies aren’t.

Inside: the forty-one-page strategy · roadmaps are wish lists · the pillar argument · target state first, then resources · best practices are past practices.

Tool: Stop Test. Voice: Sullivan & Hardy — 10x is simpler than 2x. From: Ch 2 + Ch 5.

3Delivery Is Not Valuemerged

Argues: delivery is a cost; value is a resource outcome; the gap between them is where the money goes.

Inside: the go-live party and the quarter after · value realised vs change-in-progress vs cost of change · transformation debt, the compounded cost of deferred decisions, and what it looks like (exceptions, workarounds, shadow tools) · results, not effort.

Tool: Illusion Audit. Voice: none. From: Ch 20 + Ch 22.

Part II · What Leaders Must Own
Scene 2 · Year One

Twelve months in: what went live, what stalled, which resource ran out first. Rule for the part: what a resource is, why it’s finite, what you own. Nothing about how.

4Six Resources, One Decisionmerged

Argues: every transformation draws on six resources — capital, capacity, attention, data, architecture, risk — and at any moment one of them is the decision nobody is making.

Inside: the resource radar as a scene · a page per resource: what it is, why it’s finite, what can’t be delegated (capital’s “what you own” lands here; its method waits for Ch 11) · the resources live in systems that don’t talk to each other, one section pointing to Ch 14.

Tool: The Decision You’re Avoiding. Voice: Goldratt, once — the constraint moves. From: Ch 3 + the Final chapter’s model + Ch 6/7 “what you own”.

5Change Capacity Is Finiterestored

Argues: the capacity of people to absorb change is finite and biological, and nobody is counting it.

Inside: the team on its fourth system in three years · the change fatigue loop · unplanned change as the hidden draw on capacity · capacity governed, not assumed · change practitioners as governed capacity · a fair hearing for Kotter and Heath.

Tool: Change Capacity Kill List. Voice: Kahneman, carried from v0.6. From: Ch 18 + Ch 7 + Ch 4.

6The Shape of Future Costsmerged

Argues: data, architecture and risk are three faces of one resource — what your past decisions will cost you next year.

Inside: the customer with four definitions · definitions are policy · if BI isn’t trusted, AI automates disagreement · architecture as the record of past shortcuts · the risk we designed vs the risks from outside · options kept open.

Tool: The Risk We Designed. Voice: none. From: Ch 9 + Ch 10 + Ch 11. May split in two if the draft runs long.

7Attention: The Constraint Is Youmerged

Argues: the scarcest resource is the leadership team’s attention, and the queue of undecided questions is where transformations stall.

Inside: fourteen green dashboards · the chart-of-accounts decision that took eleven weeks, told in full · the calendar audit, 40% / 4% · decision latency as the number to watch · things get stuck because doing hasn’t been decided.

Tool: Attention Leak Test. Voice: Martell. From: Ch 8 + the decision-quality core of Ch 12. Ends the part by turning the lens on the reader.

Part III · The Discipline — DECIDE
Scene 3 · The Reckoning

The meeting where the portfolio gets cut. DECIDE laid out as six verbs on one page, the only place the method is shown whole. Rule: method only; the resources are not re-argued.

8DeclaremergedDecide what must win, and who owns it

Argues: intent is a decision with an owner, not a slogan.

Inside: the ownership vacuum · decision tickets · the what and the why, never the how · culture and OKRs · business ownership is non-negotiable.

Tool: Ownership Vacuum Audit. Voice: Who Not How. From: Ch 12 + Ch 14.

9ExposeSee everything the enterprise is trying to do

Argues: you can’t decide about demand you can’t see; one portfolio, not five.

Inside: the day the shadow list appears · one intake · the last three things you stopped · letting go of the 80 percent · the Portfolio Health Check.

Tool: 20-Minute Portfolio Cut. Voice: Allen — capture everything. From: Ch 13.

10ConstrainmergedSequence it, or suffocate

Argues: capacity is a limit you respect by sequencing, not a problem you solve by hiring.

Inside: WIP limits at enterprise scale · the middle manager’s week · simplification as a strategic act · the art of quitting.

Tool: Friction Flush. Voice: none. From: Ch 19 + the sequencing half of Ch 18.

11InvestmergedFund fully, or not at all

Argues: funding is a statement of intent; a half-funded initiative is a decision not to do it, made expensively.

Inside: 60% across fourteen — Elena’s spreadsheet · learning tranches released on evidence · pilots vs readiness.

Tool: The Recommitment Line. Voice: none. From: Ch 6 + Ch 17’s pilots argument.

12DefendrestoredVendors don’t own your strategy. You do.

Argues: the decision you declared in Ch 8 will be relitigated weekly — by vendors, by the steering committee, by the loudest function — and defending it is the leader’s job, not the PMO’s.

Inside: relitigation patterns · the vendor reset script · what you can and cannot control · a fair hearing for the vendor’s side.

Tool: Reclaim Strategy Ownership. Voice: Mel Robbins — if they get offended, let them. From: Ch 15 + Ch 8’s “No.”

13EvaluateReview is not reporting

Argues: a review that cannot end in stopping something is reporting.

Inside: measure advantage, not activity — decision velocity, waste removed, reuse, automation lift · the cadence with teeth · the recommitment question, every quarter.

Tool: Red Metric Test. Voice: Allen — the weekly review. From: Ch 21 + Ch 12’s governance rhythms.

Part IV · The Enterprise That Moves
Scene 4 · Two Years On

Tool: Decision Trace Test. Voice: none. From: Ch 16 + Ch 24.

15AI Multiplies Whatever You Aremerged

Tool: Acceleration Stress Test. Voice: none. From: Ch 23 + Ch 17’s readiness half.

16The Enterprise That Movesmerged

Argues: mobility, not speed — changing direction without chaos — built from consistency, not intensity.

Inside: Two Years On, resolved · the operating model as the synthesis: six resources, DECIDE, one system · the ninety-day start · only that which can change can continue.

Tool: The Shock Test. Voice: Darren Hardy. From: Ch 25 + the Final chapter.
